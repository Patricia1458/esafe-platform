Quick Reference Card

The top 10 cybersecurity habits, worth keeping close.

    Glossary

    Phishing
    A deceptive email designed to trick you into clicking a malicious link, opening an infected attachment, or revealing credentials by impersonating a trusted sender.
    Vishing
    Phishing carried out over a phone call, where an attacker impersonates a trusted party such as IT support or a bank to extract information.
    Smishing
    Phishing carried out via SMS text message, often creating urgency around a package, payment, or account issue.
    Social Engineering
    The use of psychological manipulation — urgency, authority, trust, fear — to trick someone into taking an action or revealing information.
    Ransomware
    Malicious software that encrypts your files and demands payment for the decryption key.
    Trojan
    Malware disguised as legitimate software that performs malicious actions once installed.
    Malware
    A general term for any software designed to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorised access to a system.
    MFA (Multi-Factor Authentication)
    A login method requiring two or more proofs of identity, such as a password plus a code from your phone.
    VPN (Virtual Private Network)
    A service that encrypts your internet connection, protecting your data on untrusted networks such as public WiFi.
    Credential Stuffing
    An attack where stolen username and password pairs from one breach are automatically tried against other services.
    Pretexting
    Creating a fabricated scenario or false identity to manipulate someone into providing information or access.
    Tailgating
    Following an authorised person through a secured door without badging in yourself.
    Shadow IT
    Software, apps, or cloud tools used for work without approval or oversight from the IT department.
    Least Privilege
    The principle of giving people access only to the systems and data they need for their specific role, nothing more.
    Data Exfiltration
    The unauthorised transfer of data out of an organisation, often the goal of a cyberattack or insider threat.
    Insider Threat
    A security risk that originates from someone with legitimate access, such as a current or former employee.
    CSP (Content Security Policy)
    A browser security feature that restricts which sources of content a website is allowed to load, reducing the risk of malicious script injection.
    XSS (Cross-Site Scripting)
    An attack where malicious script is injected into a website and executed in another user's browser.
    Typosquatting
    Registering a domain name that closely resembles a legitimate one to trick users into visiting a fake site.
    Man in the Middle
    An attack where someone secretly intercepts and possibly alters communication between two parties who believe they are communicating directly.

    Incident Reporting

    Report immediately — even if you are not sure

    If something feels wrong — an unexpected email, a strange call, unusual system behaviour, a lost device — report it right away. It is always better to report something that turns out to be nothing than to stay silent about a real threat.
